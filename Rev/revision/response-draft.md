## Comment 1

The manuscript states that two selected road sections capture all positive accessibility benefits and that larger portfolios provide no additional benefit. The described procedure appears to select sections sequentially according to their current marginal benefits, recalculating accessibility after each selection. Such rerouting accounts for interactions involving previously selected sections, but it does not guarantee identification of beneficial combinations among unselected sections. Two sections with zero individual benefit may jointly restore access. Consequently, a plateau along the selected sequence does not establish that all alternative portfolios are ineffective.

## Comment 2

Higher hazard-evidence confidence increases the priority score. However, stronger documentation of an event does not necessarily indicate greater physical severity or greater humanitarian need. This distinction matters because optical-data availability varies spatially. Treating unavailable optical observations as missing rather than negative is appropriate, but it does not by itself demonstrate that observation availability has no influence on confidence classification. The authors should explain why evidence confidence should increase intervention priority, assess its relationship with data availability, and clearly present rankings obtained without this component.

## Comment 2

2. The conversion of the hazard footprint into road closures is central to all subsequent results. The manuscript states that road edges are removed according to the hazard footprint and closure rule, but it does not sufficiently demonstrate how a road intersecting the footprint is classified as destroyed or closed. Since field observations of road damage were unavailable, please clarify the exact closure criteria, the treatment of bridges and partially affected road sections, and the evidence used to assign road status.

**Response:**
Thank you for highlighting the need to make the closure criteria reproducible. The revised Data and Methodology sections specify the CEMS-to-OSM matching tolerances, bridge association, most-severe-grade rule, and joint conditions for removing an edge, including the treatment of partially intersecting sections. CEMS supplies the mapped damage grades; footprint intersection selects which graded edges enter each modeled closure scenario. The Results and Figure 2 caption now identify the full-edge closure units and report their extent.

"CEMS EMSR927 grades are linked to OSM edges in EPSG:32645 using 20 m buffers around road lines and, for OSM-tagged bridge edges, the nearest CEMS bridge point within 100 m; 700 of 706 road features and 18 of 32 bridge points match the graph."

## Comment 5

5. The intervention-priority score equally weights hazard-evidence class, assigned population, and accessibility severity after converting them to tied midranks. This is transparent, but equal weighting remains a decision choice rather than an empirically established relationship with disaster risk or recovery needs. Please justify the use of equal weights and explain the practical meaning of the resulting score. In particular, clarify how settlements with strong hazard evidence but little modeled access loss compare with settlements having weaker hazard evidence but complete isolation. The sensitivity analysis is useful, but stability under selected weights does not independently validate the ranking. A comparison with observed impacts, verified access conditions, or an independent prioritization method would strengthen the conclusions.
